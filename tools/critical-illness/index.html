How it works

A serious illness brings a hospital bill. Medical insurance pays the hospital bill first, up to its yearly limit. Your critical illness cover, a lump sum paid on diagnosis, and your emergency fund pay the rest of the bill.

What is left replaces your income while you recover, one month at a time, until it runs out. The months are what is left divided by the income you would need each month. No return and no inflation are counted.

If the bill is bigger than everything set against it, the part nothing covers is a shortfall, and nothing is left to replace your income.

A worked example

A household earns ₱100,000 a month. Its critical illness cover pays ₱1,000,000 on diagnosis and its medical insurance pays up to ₱400,000 a year. It keeps ₱100,000 for emergencies. The illness brings a ₱1,000,000 hospital bill.

    How to use your results

    If part of the bill is not covered, that is the first gap to close. It would come out of other savings or new debt. A higher medical insurance limit or a larger critical illness cover closes it.

    Once the bill is covered, the months are how long you could stop working. Try a larger bill to see how much room your cover leaves.
