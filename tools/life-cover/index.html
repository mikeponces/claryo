How it works

Your family needs a share of your yearly income for a number of years. The cover that pays for it is drawn down to zero over those years: the family takes a year's income at the start of each year, the rest of the money earns a return, and each year's draw rises with inflation.

Before the family draws anything, the payout clears your debts and pays for settling the estate: a funeral, the paperwork and any estate tax. Your savings and investments add to what is left. That total is what funds the family.

The cost of settling the estate sits beside the need and is never added to it. The result is how many years the money lasts, out of the years you chose.

A worked example

How to use your results

The shortfall is how much more your family would need today to fund every year you chose. More cover, more savings or fewer debts each close part of it.

On the chart, the red years are the ones your family would go without. A cover that funds every year leaves no red.

Ten times income is a common rule of thumb. The last line shows how far it would go for your household, with your own numbers.
