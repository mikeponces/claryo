How it works

Your investment rate is what you invest each month divided by your monthly income. At or above your target, the rate is on track. At half the target or more, it needs attention. Below half, it is at risk.

The projection grows what you already hold, plus your monthly contribution, at your yearly return, compounded every month. You contribute for the years you choose; after that the balance keeps growing with no new money.

The total in the year you pick is also shown in today's money, discounted by inflation. The monthly income it could pay comes from that figure in today's money, at your withdrawal rate.

    How to use your results

    Below half your target the rate is at risk. Investing a larger share of each paycheck moves it fastest.

    The projection assumes the same rate and the same return every year. A higher return or more years of contributing raise the total. Higher inflation lowers what it is worth today.

    The monthly income is what the total, in today's money, could pay each month at your withdrawal rate.
